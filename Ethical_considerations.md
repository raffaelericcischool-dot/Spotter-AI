# Ethical Considerations

## Ethics section of the Spotter AI project

# **1\. Identification (The ethical issues)**

* **Clinical risk and overload (Responsibility):** Spotter AI builds nutrition and training plans by combining three sources: the athlete’s medical history, biometric data from Google Health Connect (sleep, HRV, resting heart rate, steps) and daily logs. The first risk is that the system fails to notice a hidden health problem or a state of overload. It could then suggest a calorie deficit that is too large, or a training load that is wrong for an athlete with an undiagnosed injury, with overtraining or with an eating disorder. The opposite risk is just as real. Our internal tests showed it: in test T3, on anti-inflammatory drugs, the system was set so cautiously that it banned the drugs completely, which is not correct. The threshold must therefore sit between two mistakes — taking clinical risk too lightly, and being too strict — and the agent must never take the doctor’s place.  
* **Data protection and the Telegram channel (Privacy):** The system handles data that the GDPR treats as a special category: medical reports, blood tests, continuous biometric data and information about the user’s psychological state. The main channel is Telegram, a third-party messaging app. Its standard chats do not use end-to-end encryption, and they keep the message history on the user’s device. The risk works in both directions. Data coming in includes uploaded PDFs and photographs of medical reports, as well as everyday messages; data going out includes the agent’s replies. Either can be seen by accident on a phone or a computer that is shared or left unlocked.  
* **Supplements, medicines and anti-doping rules (Responsibility):** Competitive and semi-professional athletes are subject to WADA testing. For them there is a real risk of taking a banned substance without knowing it, through a supplement that is contaminated or has never been checked. Athletes may also ask unclear questions about drugs that speed up recovery. For amateurs too, using medicines or supplements in the wrong way is a direct danger to health.  
* **Algorithmic fairness and unclear estimates (Bias and Transparency):** Language models and nutrition databases can carry demographic bias. Open Food Facts, for example, is filled in by its own users, and models are often calibrated on physiological data that is mostly male. The nutritional values themselves can also be inaccurate. There is a second problem. When an LLM estimates the macronutrients in a home-cooked meal, the athlete may read the figure as hard clinical data rather than as an estimate.

# **2\. Analysis (The issues in our specific context)**

* **User profile and the information gap:** Spotter AI is aimed at a wide range of athletes: amateurs, semi-professionals and professionals who have no dedicated team around them — no sports doctor, no nutritionist, no psychologist. Because they have no specialist to turn to, these users tend to treat the agent’s suggestions not as general information but as instructions to follow exactly. A short written disclaimer (‘this is not medical advice’) is therefore not enough on its own. Nor is a general invitation to see a specialist, unless the system also blocks the features that could cause harm.  
* **How sensitive the data is, and how it is used:** Athletes share very private information: emotional low points, joint pain, blood test results and, for female athletes, data about the menstrual cycle. The system uses this to put recovery and training load in context. It makes no diagnosis of any kind. Under Article 9 of the GDPR this counts as health and biometric data. If someone gained access to it on a shared device, or if it were stored without protection, the effect on the athlete’s privacy and freedom of choice would be serious.

# **3\. Design Response (What we built in response)**

* **Automated ethical oversight and operating thresholds (Responsibility):** Every reply put together by the Orchestrator must pass through the Ethical Agent, which checks it against the athlete’s profile and raw data. The reply can be regenerated up to three times. If it is refused a third time, the system sends a safe fallback message: rest, stop any intensive plan, see a doctor. The system also applies clear thresholds. It blocks a nutrition plan if the calorie deficit is more than 20–25% below estimated needs. It blocks a training load that does not match a sharp fall in HRV or sleep compared with the athlete’s own baseline, and it takes BMI into account as well. Some situations stop the system completely: acute pain, signs of an eating disorder, or serious psychological distress. This includes any sign of self-harm — in test T3 the system sent the user straight to the emergency number 112\. In these cases, it suspends every diet and training instruction, instead of simply showing a warning.  
* **Policy on medicines, supplements and WADA (Responsibility):** The Ethical Agent and the Nutrition Agent apply a zero-tolerance policy to doping substances and to prescribing drugs. The system does not prescribe medicines and does not set doses. It explains the limits of its own role instead, without issuing absolute medical bans that have no basis. It recommends no food supplements at all, and nutrition plans are built from ordinary foods only. When a user asks about medicines or supplements, the agent does not search external databases, which are not connected yet. It asks the user to check the official WADA list themselves and to see a sports doctor.  
* **Telegram security, pseudonymisation and the GDPR (Privacy):** To protect data travelling in both directions on Telegram, the architecture works on several levels:  
  * Explicit consent is required before any medical report is stored.  
  * Raw files (PDFs and images of medical reports) are deleted automatically and immediately from the n8n transit servers, as soon as the LLM has extracted the values. The user is also asked to delete sensitive documents from the Telegram chat history and to turn on the passcode lock in the app.  
  * Data is pseudonymised before it is sent to external LLMs. The name, the Telegram username and any direct identifiers are removed and replaced with an internal technical ID. This keeps the link with the athlete’s personal history without showing their identity to outside AI providers.  
  * Long-term storage happens only in an encrypted Supabase database, hosted in the European Union and protected by Row-Level Security (RLS). Users can see or delete all of their data whenever they want.

To reduce this risk, we are developing an extra workflow, now in beta, that connects WhatsApp, whose chats use end-to-end encryption.

* **Reducing bias and being transparent with users (Bias and Transparency):** General-purpose models and databases carry bias. To work against it, the agents compare each athlete with their own historical baseline rather than with population averages when they assess recovery, HRV and sleep. The Sleep Agent in particular works this way. They also adapt their advice to the athlete’s age, sex and sport. On transparency, the bot says clearly that it is an AI assistant. It always names the source behind its advice: data from the wearable device, Open Food Facts codes, guidelines from the knowledge base, or SearXNG results. It marks the macronutrients of home-cooked meals as rough estimates. And it lets the athlete ask why: ‘Why are you recommending this?’

# **4\. Honest Limitations (What the system cannot do yet)**

* **Automated checking, and trends over several weeks:** At the moment the Ethical Agent’s checks are fully automated through an LLM. No human reviews them in real time before the reply goes out (human-in-the-loop). There is a second gap. Supabase keeps the long-term history and the Sleep Agent uses the individual baseline, but the system still has no module for the statistical analysis of trends over several weeks. Conditions that build up slowly, such as RED-S (Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport) or growing burnout, can therefore escape a check that looks only at single daily exchanges.  
* **No direct link to drug databases or to WADA:** The system is not yet connected by API to certified drug databases, or to the current WADA list. It therefore cannot automatically check active ingredients, drug interactions, or whether the athlete needs a therapeutic use exemption (TUE), against their medical history.
